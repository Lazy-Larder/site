  • Your right of access - You have the right to ask us for copies of your personal information. You can request other information such as details about where we get personal information from and who we share personal information with. There are some exemptions which means you may not receive all the information you ask for. Read more about the right of access.
  • Your right to rectification - You have the right to ask us to correct or delete personal information you think is inaccurate or incomplete. Read more about the right to rectification.
  • Your right to erasure - You have the right to ask us to delete your personal information. Read more about the right to erasure.
  • Your right to restriction of processing - You have the right to ask us to limit how we can use your personal information. Read more about the right to restriction of processing.
  • Your right to object to processing - You have the right to object to the processing of your personal data. Read more about the right to object to processing.
  • Your right to data portability - You have the right to ask that we transfer the personal information you gave us to another organisation, or to you. Read more about the right to data portability.
  • Your right to withdraw consent – When we use consent as our lawful basis you have the right to withdraw your consent at any time. Read more about the right to withdraw consent.

Our lawful bases for the collection and use of your data

Our lawful bases for collecting or using personal information for the operation of customer accounts and guarantees are:

Legitimate interests – we’re collecting or using your information because it benefits you, our organisation or someone else, without causing an undue risk of harm to anyone. All of your data protection rights may apply, except the right to portability. Our legitimate interests are:

Legitimate interests cover two narrower uses within account operation: identifying content a user personally created, so it can be removed when erasing their account; and identifying the pseudonymised account behind a report, or behind reported content, to enforce the Terms on reporting and removing content that breaches them. This lets users control their own data precisely and keeps shared content safe to use, without processing more account information than these purposes need.

For more information on our use of legitimate interests as a lawful basis you can contact us using the contact details set out above.

Our lawful bases for collecting or using personal information for Attribute authorship are:

Legitimate interests – we’re collecting or using your information because it benefits you, our organisation or someone else, without causing an undue risk of harm to anyone. All of your data protection rights may apply, except the right to portability. Our legitimate interests are:

Benefit: retaining and showing a linked, third-party content's original author name gives proper credit, in line with the Terms’ own rule against presenting someone else’s content as original content.

Necessity: there’s no practical alternative - the name is already published by the author alongside the content, and showing it in the service is the only way to carry that same attribution through.

Balancing: only the name is retained, already made public by the author for this exact purpose, and it isn’t used beyond attribution. This is a narrow, low-risk use that matches what the author already intended by publishing under their name, ensuring it doesn’t place this benefit above the author’s own interest in how their name is used.

For more information on our use of legitimate interests as a lawful basis you can contact us using the contact details set out above.

Our lawful bases for collecting or using personal information for content sharing are:

Legitimate interests – we’re collecting or using your information because it benefits you, our organisation or someone else, without causing an undue risk of harm to anyone. All of your data protection rights may apply, except the right to portability. Our legitimate interests are:

Benefit: retaining an account identifier on shared content, and on any report made against it, increases usability and lets the sharing feature work as intended - the creator can manage or remove their own shared content, and content or accounts that breach the Terms can be identified and acted on. Including a photo with shared content, where the creator has added one, gives the people receiving it a clearer, more useful sense of the content - a clear benefit to sharing.

Necessity: for the account identifier, there’s no practical alternative - without it, there’d be no way to tell who created the content, or act on a report flagging content that breaches Lazy Larder’s T&Cs. Photos are not required.

Balancing: the identifier is pseudonymised, not a name or contact detail, and cannot be resolved back to a person except by Apple. Any photo used is one the creator chose to add to their own recipe. Our T&Cs require only images of food, so sharing it carries no more risk than sharing the content itself. Neither is used for profiling, so this is a narrow, low-risk use that doesn’t place these benefits above the interests of the people whose content or reports it relates to.

Where we get personal information from

  • Directly from you
  • Publicly available sources
  • Third parties:

Lazy Larder utilises services provided by Apple Inc., including: Sign in with Apple account identity; iCloud / CloudKit storage, sync and sharing of app content between household members; push notification delivery; and subscription and entitlement status via the App Store, with no card, bank, or billing details shared with the app.

How long we keep information

  • Account identity (Apple ID name) is never stored — it’s resolved live from Apple each time it’s needed.
  • Recipes, shopping lists, and other content are kept for as long as the account exists. Using Erase All Data starts a 14-day grace period, after which everything is permanently deleted.
  • Content shared with others via a code is kept for as long as it remains in active use; shares that go unused for an extended period are periodically reviewed and may be removed.
  • Reports made about shared content are kept for as long as the reporting account exists, and for a further period afterwards, to prevent an account circumventing enforcement action by leaving and rejoining.
  • Subscription status is kept while a subscription is active, and for a limited period afterwards for support purposes.

For more information on how long we store your personal information or the criteria we use to determine this please contact us using the details provided above.
